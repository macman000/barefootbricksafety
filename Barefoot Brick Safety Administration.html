What is a brick incident?

A brick incident happens when a bare or socked foot comes down on a small plastic building brick with the full weight of a human body behind it.

Toy bricks are designed to hold their shape under pressure. That makes them excellent for building castles, and terrible for standing on. The weight of an adult, concentrated on a few tiny studs, produces a level of pain that scientists describe as “why.”

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt so much?

Your foot is soft and spread out. A brick is hard and pointy in six places. When you step on it, all of you is pushing on a tiny area, and the brick refuses to give up even a little.

Does the brick ever break?

No. The brick wins every time. It will be fine. You will not be fine for about forty-five seconds.

Are socks enough protection?

Socks reduce pain by roughly 4%, which is not enough. They also make it easier to slide onto a second brick.

Is it okay to say a bad word?

The BBSA neither supports nor discourages this. We do recommend checking whether children are in the room first.

Which brick is the worst one?

See the Pain Index. Officially it is the small sloped roof piece. Unofficially, it is whichever one you just stepped on.

Can I sue the brick?

You may, but bricks have no money and excellent lawyers.

Is this a real government agency?

No. But the pain is real.

About the BBSA

The Barefoot Brick Safety Administration was founded in 1958*, shortly after the first plastic brick was left on a floor and the first adult stepped on it in the dark.

Our mission is simple: a nation where everyone can walk to the kitchen at night without fear. Until that day comes, we offer training, research, and a place to report what happened to you.

*Probably. Records were lost when someone stepped on a brick and dropped the filing box.

The Barefoot Brick Safety Administration is not a real agency, and is not connected with any toy company. Please put your bricks away.
